HUD mode

Your phone as a heads-up display for your car: lay it flat on the dashboard and read your GPS speed on the windscreen.

GPS, in km/h or mph

How it works

  1. 1

    Open HUD mode: tap HUD mode on Home or in Styles, use the HUD mode tile in Quick Settings, or long-press the app's icon. The first time, a short setup asks one question per step.

  2. 2

    Pick your look: Number, Gauge arc, Fighter or Centre bar, drawn by Horizon (free), Glass, Pulse or Vector, in the style's own colour or in Lime, Cyan, Ice white, Amber or Violet.

  3. 3

    Say how the phone lies: top edge to the glass, top edge to you, or upright in a holder. Lying flat, your speed is drawn flipped, so the windscreen reflects it the right way round; in a holder it reads straight, with no flip.

  4. 4

    While parked, lay the phone flat on the dashboard in front of the wheel, screen up, where it can't slide and clear of the airbags. Then drive and leave it alone: once you've stopped, tap it once for Exit and Flip.

What it shows

HUD mode turns your phone into a heads-up display for your car. It fills the screen with your speed alone, drawn flipped, so the windscreen in front of you reflects it the right way round: a car HUD with no extra box on the dashboard.

Four looks, four styles.

Number, Gauge arc, Fighter and Centre bar, drawn by Horizon for free, or by Glass, Pulse and Vector with the style, Speedometer Plus or a 24-hour trial.

Your colour.

Keep the style's own colour, or pick Lime, Cyan, Ice white, Amber or Violet, whichever reads best on your glass.

More on the glass.

Add the clock, the trip, your heading and a speed warning: above the limit you set, the speed turns red and the phone beeps once. The app doesn't know the road's limit.

Opens in your car.

Switch on Open by itself in my car and pick your car once: HUD mode opens when the phone joins its Bluetooth.

Looks after your phone.

On a low battery a small Plug in mark shows once per drive. If the phone gets hot, HUD mode dims, then pauses to let it cool down, and comes back by itself.

Seeing double?

A windscreen has two glass surfaces, and each one reflects the phone, so a bare windscreen shows your speed twice. A HUD film on the inside of the glass, or a HUD reflector plate on the dashboard, leaves one sharp image.

Before you drive

Set it up while parked

Lay the phone where it can't slide, clear of the airbags, and don't touch it while you drive. Check that your country allows a phone on the dashboard.

Good to know

  • HUD mode reads best at dusk, at night and on cloudy days. In bright sun the reflection is faint.
  • It has no menus and no ads while you drive: one tap shows Exit and Flip.
  • Your speed comes from GPS while HUD mode is open, and it never leaves the phone. Until the phone has a fix, the glass shows NO GPS.
  • Each HUD style's page has an Original | HUD switch: Horizon, Glass, Pulse and Vector.
