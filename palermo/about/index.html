About Palermo Noir

What Palermo Noir is

Palermo Noir is a hidden-role party game for 6 to 14 friends in one room: every phone hides a secret card, and the TV runs the town. A few of you are killers who know each other. Everyone else has to find them by talking, before the killers are as many as the rest.

  • Nobody sits outA noir narrator leads each night and each morning, so the friend who used to run the game gets a card too. Later the first player out narrates the mornings, a Greek house rule kept on purpose.
  • Secrets stay secretNo phone makes a sound, every screen looks the same after dark, and your card stays hidden unless you press and hold.
  • One screen for everyoneAny TV with a web browser, or a laptop plugged into one, shows the town: the dawn's news, the rumour, every accusation as it lands and the vote.

How a round goes

After the deal and a first night where the killers only meet, every round runs the same way, until one side wins.

  • The nightIn secret, the killers settle on a victim, the Doctor guards someone and the Detective checks a suspect, while everyone else says whom they trust.
  • DawnThe narrator announces who died in the night, or that nobody did, and the day begins.
  • Talk and voteThe town argues out loud. Each player points at a suspect from their phone, and whoever draws the most arrows has half a minute to answer before the vote.
  • The verdictA tie sends the tied players to a revote. Whoever goes out leaves the table, and night falls again.

What's inside

  • 14 rolesFrom the Known Killer and the Hidden Killer to the Seer, the Madman and the Kamikaze, each with its own card and its own voice.
  • The Lawyer's briefcase17 power cards: the Lawyer keeps 2 of the 5 dealt at random, and each one works once.
  • Rules for every groupStart from Classic, Quick, Full house or Random, then set the timers, open or secret votes, ties and reveals before the deal.
  • English or GreekWhichever the host picks, narration, cards and screens all switch to it.
  • A newsreel from 1947Catch the game's first killer and the TV screens the arrest as a newsreel, filmed on a street of 1947 Palermo.
  • Share the nightAt the end each phone can turn the game into your own card, a film poster, the town paper's front page or the whole table.

Questions

What is the town's rumour?

Every night, besides their role's move, each player names the one they suspect most. At dawn the TV shows the name picked most often as the town's rumour, so the day's talk starts from a hint.

Who wins?

The town, once every killer is out. The killers, once they are as many as everyone else still alive. A few roles keep their own score: the Madman wins alone if the town votes him out.

What if nobody dies for a while?

After three full rounds without a death, the narrator tells the town it won't wait any longer, and from then on fate settles every tie.

Can the dead still play along?

The first player out becomes the night announcer: their phone shows every role and every night's moves, and they choose the lines for the dawn. A host rule lets every dead player see it all.
