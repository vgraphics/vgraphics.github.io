This website

Hosting

vgraphics.gr is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. Your browser's request for each page reaches GitHub with your IP address, the page's address and your browser's name and version. GitHub logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, whether or not they have a GitHub account (GitHub Pages: data collection). We don't get these logs. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

No accounts, cookies or trackers

The website has no accounts and no sign-in of its own. Apart from the play pages of the web games (see ads and statistics) and the apps' try pages (see trying an app), no page here sets cookies, counts statistics or shows ads, and none loads scripts or fonts from another site: the fonts and pictures come from vgraphics.gr itself.

The live Exitwise ranking

The home, services and multiplayer pages and the Exitwise ranking show Exitwise's live leaderboards. VHub, our player network, keeps them: your browser reads them straight from VHub's public leaderboards in Google Cloud Firestore, and the players' pictures from VHub's Cloud Storage, both part of Google's Firebase (firestore.googleapis.com and firebasestorage.googleapis.com), so Google receives your IP address and browser details for those requests. No cookie is set, no sign-in is needed, and nothing else about you is sent. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Email

When you email us, we get your address and what you write, and we use them only to answer you. Our email runs on Gmail, so Google stores the messages for us. We keep them as long as we need them for what you asked.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a secure website that works, shows the live ranking and answers your messages (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

Ads in the web games

The web games on vgraphics.gr can show ads from Google AdSense on their play pages, and nowhere else: today that is Exitwise in your browser, and future web games will work the same way. No other page shows ads.

When ads load

  • A play page first reads the site's ad switch, /assets/web-ads.js, from vgraphics.gr itself. Only when it turns ads on for that game does the page load Google's ad code. While it is off, nothing loads from Google for ads, and there are no ads and no ad cookies. The one exception is the page's ad test mode, which we open by adding ?adtest=on to its address: it loads Google's ad code to show test ads.
  • With ads on, a game shows a banner under the game from the first level. Once Google allows them for the site, it also shows occasional full-screen ads between levels, and reward videos that you choose to watch, for example to continue a level.
  • If an ad blocker stops Google's ad code, the game carries on without ads.

Cookies

Google, and other ad companies whose ads Google shows through AdSense, use cookies and similar technologies to show ads, measure them, limit how often you see the same ad and prevent fraud.

  • Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your earlier visits to this website or other websites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies lets it and its partners serve ads to you based on your visits to this website and/or other sites on the internet.
  • You can opt out of personalised advertising in Google's Ad Settings. You can also opt out of many other ad companies' use of cookies for personalised ads at aboutads.info or youronlinechoices.eu.

Google explains its part in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and in the Google Privacy Policy.

Your choices

  • European Economic Area, UK and Switzerland: the first time you open a play page with ads on, Google's consent message asks whether Google and the ad companies it lists may use cookies and your data for ads. Ads are only personalised if you agree. If you don't, you may still see ads, but they are chosen from the page and your general location instead. Change your choice at any time with Ad choices under the game.
  • US states with a privacy law: Google's message shows a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link on the page, where you can opt out of the sale or sharing of your data for ads.
  • Everywhere else: no message asks first. With ads on, Google and the ad companies it works with may use cookies for ads, personalised ones included, from your first visit. You can opt out at any time with the links under Cookies, above.

Legal basis: where the law requires consent, as in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, ad cookies and personalised ads rely on your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time.

Statistics in the web games

The play pages can count game statistics with Google Analytics: for example the levels players finish, where they get stuck, and error reports when something goes wrong in the game. We use them only to improve the games.

  • Only if you allow it. The first time a game opens, it asks, for example "Help improve Exitwise?", with Allow and No thanks. If Google's consent message (above) has already asked you about analytics, the game uses that answer instead of asking again.
  • Nothing is loaded from Google Analytics or sent to it before you allow it, and the game works the same either way.
  • If you allow it, Google Analytics keeps an identifier in a cookie in your browser. It doesn't get your name, email address or Google account.
  • Your browser remembers your answer (in its local storage), so the game doesn't ask again. Change it at any time with Statistics under the game. If you withdraw it, the page reloads without Google Analytics.

No other page on vgraphics.gr counts statistics, apart from the apps' try pages (below), which ask the same way. What each game counts is in its own policy: Exitwise: analytics and crash reports.

Legal basis: your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)).

Trying an app in your browser

An app's try page runs the app itself in your browser: today that is Speedometer Live Wallpaper, live in your browser. It shows no ads, and everything it runs comes from vgraphics.gr itself.

  • Its readings stay in your browser. To fill its gauges, the page reads your browser's clock and time zone and, where your browser offers them, your battery's level and charging and the kind of network you're on. Nothing it reads is sent anywhere.
  • Your location only if you ask. Use my real speed asks your browser for your location, and your browser asks you first. The page uses it only to work out your speed while it's open, and never sends or keeps it. Back to the demo drive stops it.
  • Your last look. Your browser remembers the style and settings you looked at last (in its local storage), so the page opens on them next time. A link to a look carries it after the # in its address, which browsers don't send to us.
  • Statistics only if you allow them. A try page that counts statistics asks first, as the web games do (above): for example "Help improve Speedometer Live Wallpaper?". It would count which styles and settings you look at, how fast the page loads, and errors. Change your answer at any time with Statistics on the page.

Legal basis: the readings and your location are used only in your browser, at your request (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)); statistics rely on your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)).

VGraphics Deployer

VGraphics Deployer is the internal tool we use to release our games and apps and to follow their statistics. It isn't a public app: it runs on VGraphics' own computer.

Who signs in

Only VGraphics' own staff sign in to it with Google, and only with VGraphics' own Google account. It never asks players, visitors or anyone else to sign in.

What it may read

When we sign in, Google asks us to allow read-only access to two things, and the Deployer asks for nothing else:

  • AdMob reports: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/admob.readonly
  • AdSense reports: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/adsense.readonly

With them it reads the estimated earnings and ad impressions of VGraphics' own apps and website, per day, ad format and ad unit, and the review status of our sites in AdSense. These are totals: they say nothing about the people who saw the ads. The Deployer can't change anything in AdMob or AdSense, and it doesn't ask for the account's name, email address or any other Google data.

Where it stays

  • The sign-in that Google gives the Deployer, and the reports it reads, are kept on VGraphics' own computer. Each report is kept only until a newer copy replaces it.
  • They are not shared with anyone, not sold, and not used for advertising.

Removing its access

The Deployer's access can be removed at any time from the Google Account that signed in: open Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services (myaccount.google.com/connections), choose VGraphics Deployer and delete the connection. From then on its saved sign-in no longer works, and it can't read any more reports.

VGraphics Deployer's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
