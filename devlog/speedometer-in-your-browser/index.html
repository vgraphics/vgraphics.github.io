The real Speedometer, running in your browser

27 October 2026

When we added a way to try Speedometer Live Wallpaper online, there were three options: record a video of it, draw a look-alike for the web, or run the app itself. A video can't be tapped, and a look-alike drifts further from the real thing with every update. So the try page runs the app's own drawing code.

One renderer, two homes

Every style in the app, from Classic to Harbour, draws with Android's graphics classes: a canvas, paints, paths and gradients. We moved the styles into a Kotlin Multiplatform module that draws through a thin layer with the same class names. On a phone, that layer hands each call straight to Android. In the browser, it hands it to Skia, the graphics library Chrome itself draws with, compiled to WebAssembly.

The styles never know which one they are talking to, so there is no second version of them to keep in step.

Proving nothing changed

The app's tests keep reference pictures of the styles in their different states: calibrating, charging, on Wi-Fi, at speed. The rule for the move was strict. Every one of those pictures had to come out byte for byte identical, and a style that shifted by a single pixel meant the move wasn't finished.

What a browser can read

On your phone the wallpaper reads the battery, the charger, the network and the processor. In a browser it uses what the browser is willing to share: your clock, in your time zone and with your 12 or 24-hour setting; the battery level and charging, in browsers that share them, such as Chrome; and, if you tap Use my real speed and allow it, your speed from GPS. Your location never leaves your browser.

Everything a browser keeps to itself, like the processor's clock or the phone signal, comes from a short recorded drive, the same one the app plays in its own previews.

The one thing it can't do

A web page can't set your wallpaper. That last step belongs to the app, so the page shows a QR code and a Google Play link. Every style you try on the page is in the app; the premium ones are a one-time purchase.

Why it was worth it

You can try a premium style live, with its layouts and colours, before you pay for it. And because the page runs the same code, what you see there is what you get on your home screen.
