One account across our games: what we learned building VHub

3 November 2026

Exitwise™ started life with accounts of its own: its own name screen, its own picture upload, its own leaderboards. That was fine for one game. With Speedometer Live Wallpaper getting sign-in and more games on the way, it meant a player could end up with a different name, picture and history in every product we make. So in October the accounts moved out of the games and into VHub, one player account for all of them. These are our notes from the move.

What VHub keeps, and what each game keeps

The line we drew is simple. Everything about you as a player lives in VHub: your name, your picture, your level and XP, your achievements, your friends and the global leaderboards. Everything about a single game stays with that game: your saved progress, the per-level boards, rooms and custom maps live in the game's own database, filed under the same player ID that VHub gave you.

When you sign in, you sign in to VHub, with your Google account. VHub then hands the game a session for that same ID. No game creates players of its own any more, so there is one of you, not one per product. Change your picture once and every game shows the new one.

Signing in is still optional. Every game and app works without it, and the sign-in prompt can always be skipped.

XP for a puzzle game, and for a wallpaper

One level across all our products sounds easy until you try to give XP for a live wallpaper. Exitwise is played: you win levels, finish the daily challenge, publish maps and play matches in rooms, and each of those earns XP, 20 for a level won and 40 for a daily challenge, for example. Speedometer Live Wallpaper isn't played in that sense. It sits on your home screen.

So the wallpaper earns XP for what using it means: a day with the wallpaper on, a style you collect, a layout you build. The daily caps are far apart on purpose: up to 4,000 XP a day from Exitwise, up to 200 from Speedometer, because an evening of hard puzzles and a wallpaper that is simply switched on shouldn't count the same. Every rule is written out on the leaderboards page, because a level means little if nobody can see how it is earned.

Your level, worn on your picture

A number next to a name is easy to miss, so since 29 October your picture can wear a frame that shows your rank wherever it appears in Exitwise: on leaderboards, player cards, rooms and match history. There are 13 of them, from a plain wooden ring at level 5 to a winged one at level 275. Each unlocks as your level rises, and you choose which one to wear, or none, with Edit my badge on your VHub profile. Because the picture and the frame both live in VHub, the same frame also shows on the hub, on the vgraphics.gr ranking and beside the Speedometer layouts you share.

Moving the players who were already there

Before the switch we had to decide who to bring across from Exitwise. Most of its accounts turned out to be our own: test runs, automated runs that took store screenshots, and guests who opened the game once and never finished a level. Copying all of them would have filled the new leaderboards with robots.

So only real players moved: anyone who had played, signed in, chosen a name or held coins. Their names, pictures, XP and places on the boards came with them. A guest who was left out and comes back simply gets a fresh account. Our own test and robot accounts stayed out, and were deleted only after the list of them had been checked by hand.

What broke

On 1 November we noticed that the Exitwise ranking showed none of the new players who had just found the game on Android. The cause was a single name. The Android app asked VHub to sign players in using the full name of the request, and VHub, expecting the short form, didn't recognise it and turned every request from Android away. Android players could still play, but since the update that brought VHub sign-in to Android in late October, none of the new ones had got a VHub account, a row on the boards or their settings synced.

The fix was small: VHub now accepts both forms, and it was live the same evening, with the missing players added to the boards. Since 2 November a new player also reaches the boards the moment they join, and Play Games players show under their gamertag instead of a numbered placeholder. The lasting change is a check that follows a brand-new player from sign-in through to every board and the ranking, and compares what the game recorded with what VHub shows. It runs after every change that touches sign-in, saves, scores or profiles, so we no longer rely on spotting an empty ranking by eye.

Leaving

One account also means one clear way out. Each product's profile has Delete my data for that product alone, which keeps your account, name and picture. Delete my VGraphics™ account removes everything everywhere and signs you out. Both are explained on the deletion page.
